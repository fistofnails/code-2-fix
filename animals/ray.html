Ray

Fish

Rays glide across the seafloor with wide, wing-like fins, moving with surprising elegance. They use the sandy bottom as cover and often bury themselves to rest or hide from predators.

Many rays are gentle feeders and use special sensory cells to detect prey hidden beneath the sand. Their broad bodies and flat shape make them wonderfully adapted to life near the ocean floor.

  • Habitat: Sandy bottoms and reefs
  • Diet: Small fish and shellfish
  • Movement: Flaps wide fins
  • Fun fact: Some can sense buried prey with electric signals.
